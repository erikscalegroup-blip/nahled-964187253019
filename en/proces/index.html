Process

How it works

Seven steps from hand-over to weighing report. We show each one in photos from our workshop.

A glider's path through the workshop

From hand-over to weighing report

Use ← → arrow keys to move between steps

Step 1 / 7

Hand-over and inspection

We inspect the glider, document the condition of the surface and agree on the scope of work. Whatever we find under the old paint, we report during the work — not at the end.

  1. 1

    Hand-over and inspection

    We inspect the glider, document the condition of the surface and agree on the scope of work. Whatever we find under the old paint, we report during the work — not at the end.

  2. 2

    Sanding

    The old paint comes off down to the gelcoat or laminate. It is the longest and most tedious part of the whole process — and the one that decides the result.

  3. 3

    Laminating and repairs

    Cracks, dents and old repairs are dealt with now, while the surface is bare.

  4. 4

    Filling and fairing

    The profile is faired back to true. This is where it's decided whether the surface will be truly smooth.

  5. 5

    Painting

    Polyurethane paint for its flexibility — it doesn't crack — and UV resistance. In the spray booth, in a single batch.

  6. 6

    Polishing and finishing

    Mylar and teflon tapes, seals, new registration markings, reflective elements and markings to your wishes.

  7. 7

    Weighing and hand-over

    We weigh the glider; you receive complete documentation of the work and a new weighing report.

    CRS and ARC arranged on request.
